Walk into a home where the lights, music, locks, and thermostat all feel like separate islands — each with its own app, remote, or voice command — and you understand exactly the problem Brilliant set out to solve. The idea is simple: one panel on the wall that ties everything together, so there's no fumbling through five apps to set the mood for dinner and no countertop speaker cluttering the kitchen.

## A Brief History Worth Knowing

Brilliant has been one of the more talked-about names in consumer smart home technology since it launched in 2016, but the road hasn't been smooth. In May 2024, the company ran out of money and came close to shutting down entirely, putting the future of existing installations — and the cloud servers they depend on — in question. A group of private investors acquired the company that August, and Brilliant used the fresh capital to pivot away from mass-market retail (Amazon, Best Buy, Home Depot) toward selling direct and partnering with home builders, electricians, and custom integrators to pre-install systems in new construction. That shift stabilized the brand and funded a full second-generation hardware refresh.

## What the Panel Actually Does

Each Brilliant Control replaces a standard light switch with a touchscreen that acts as a centralized hub, coordinating smart lights, music, locks, and thermostats from multiple brands over your home Wi-Fi. Built into every panel:

- **Camera with a physical privacy shutter** — a slider that fully blocks the lens when you want it off.
- **Two-way video intercom** — multiple panels installed around the house function as a room-to-room intercom system.
- **Digital photo frame mode** — upload photos through the app and the idle screen becomes a display for them instead of a blank rectangle.
- **Scene control** — a single tap can trigger a "Good Night" scene that turns off downstairs lights, locks the front door, adjusts the thermostat, and stops the music.

## Gen 1 vs. Gen 2 Hardware

The second-generation panels bring a meaningfully faster processor, dual-band Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, versus 2.4 GHz only on Gen 1), and Power over Ethernet as an option alongside standard 120V AC wiring — a significant win for new-construction projects run over low-voltage cabling. The upgrade largely eliminates the lag that some original units showed under heavy load.

Wired panels still need a standard 120V AC connection with both neutral and ground wires present in the box — generally a given in homes built from the mid-1980s onward, though older properties may need an electrician to run new wiring. Brilliant also supports multi-way switching, so a panel can sit at one end of a hallway with standard or Brilliant dimmer switches at the other end controlling the same lights. For anyone who can't modify in-wall wiring — an older home or a rented apartment — the Plug-In Control Panel mounts to any wall and runs off a standard outlet via a 13-foot USB cable, no electrical work required.

## Where It Fits Against Professional Systems

Brilliant is a wireless, IP-based system running over consumer Wi-Fi, which makes it easy to install and use but puts a ceiling on customization and reliability compared with hardwired, professional-grade platforms like Crestron and Savant. Those enterprise systems handle whole-home distribution of uncompressed video, architectural-grade sound, complex climate zoning, and shading that reacts to the sun in real time — territory a consumer touchscreen isn't built for. For a large, multi-structure estate with pool controls, a home theater, and motorized shades, a professional platform is generally the better foundation; for a single-family home or apartment that wants unified control without a construction project, Brilliant is a genuinely capable, easy-to-live-with option, especially for guests and kids who've never had to learn it.

## Deciding What's Right for You

Brilliant also offers an optional subscription, Brilliant Max, that adds self-learning automation modes so the home adapts to routines without manual scene programming. Whether that's the right layer for your home — or whether a fully custom, hardwired system makes more sense for the scale of your property — depends on your wiring, your architecture, and how much you want the technology itself to disappear into the walls. If you're weighing the two, it's worth talking through the tradeoffs with an integrator before deciding which direction to build toward.
