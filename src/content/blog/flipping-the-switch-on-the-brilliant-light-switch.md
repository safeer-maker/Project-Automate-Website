The Brilliant Smart Home Control panel replaces a standard wall switch with a 5-inch touchscreen that controls lighting, music, cameras, locks, and climate from one place, with no separate hub required. For anyone tired of juggling multiple apps and voice assistants, that's an appealing pitch. Whether it's the right fit depends heavily on your home's wiring, your budget, and how much you value an all-in-one interface versus a fully invisible one.

## Models and Pricing

Brilliant's current NextGen lineup comes in four hardwired sizes, built to fit standard 1-gang through 4-gang electrical boxes:

- **1-Switch panel** (5.6" x 3.3"): built-in Alexa, camera, intercom, motion sensor — $599
- **2-Switch panel** (5.3" x 4.75"): two physical touch sliders — $649
- **3-Switch panel** (5.3" x 6.4"): three sliders, suited to open-concept entryways — $699
- **4-Switch panel** (5.3" x 8.2"): four sliders for complex multi-load areas — $749
- **PoE 2-Switch panel**: Power over Ethernet connectivity for new construction — $699

There's also a Smart Dimmer Switch ($69.99) for rooms that don't need a full touchscreen, and a Plug-In Control Panel that mounts to any wall and runs off a standard outlet — useful for renters, since it delivers the same touchscreen, Alexa, camera, and intercom features without any electrical work. A full home typically needs three to five panels to get real value from the room-to-room intercom and scene control, which puts a complete hardware install in the $2,000 to $4,000 range before installation.

One important catch: the Smart Dimmer Switch has no WiFi radio of its own. It connects to a primary Control Panel over Bluetooth mesh, so it can't function as a standalone device — you need at least one full panel in the home before a dimmer will do anything.

## Wiring and Range

For a single-pole install, physical installation is straightforward and usually takes 15 to 30 minutes for anyone comfortable with basic electrical work; the panel auto-detects line versus load wires. Multi-way (3-way, 4-way) setups are supported but require configuring one switch as the primary power source and the others as "Always On" in the app.

Before buying, confirm your electrical box has a neutral wire and a ground wire, and that you're on standard 120V AC — the system is built for North American wiring only. It does not support Magnetic Low Voltage lighting, common in high-end architectural downlights, and is rated for up to 300W incandescent/halogen or 150W LED/CFL per switch location (500W/200W on the standalone dimmer). It can handle basic on/off for ceiling fans but not variable fan speeds.

The companion dimmers and plugs talk to a control panel over Bluetooth Low Energy mesh, officially rated for 50 feet node-to-node. In practice, dense plaster, concrete, or heavy metal framing can cut that range noticeably. Plan switch placement so no device sits more than two or three rooms from another Brilliant unit.

## What It Does Well, and Where It Falls Short

The touchscreen interface is genuinely intuitive for guests and kids, who can walk up and use it without an app or a voice command. Built-in Alexa clears countertop clutter, the room-to-room intercom is a real convenience in a larger house, and the system keeps working locally — lighting schedules and motion detection included — even if your internet drops, though cloud features like Ring feeds or Sonos streaming go offline with it.

On the downside, outfitting a whole home is expensive at $599 to $749 per panel, a glowing screen in every room can clash with a minimalist interior, the built-in speaker sounds noticeably thin for anything beyond voice feedback, and the dimmer's dependency on a full control panel limits how cheaply you can expand the system.

On integrations, Brilliant covers a lot of ground out of the box: Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit for voice; Sonos for audio; Ring and Nest for cameras, with live video popping up on the panel when your doorbell rings; Ecobee, Honeywell, and Nest for climate; and Philips Hue, LIFX, TP-Link Kasa, and Samsung SmartThings for third-party lighting and hub bridging. It's an indoor-only product, though, so outdoor spaces need a separate system.

## Renters vs. Homeowners

For renters, the Plug-In Panel with companion Smart Plugs sidesteps lease restrictions entirely and travels with you when you move. For homeowners doing new construction or a major renovation, the wired NextGen panels, PoE options included, offer a more permanent, integrated solution.

If you like what Brilliant does but want the same convenience without a screen on every wall, that's a conversation worth having — there are ways to get whole-home control with hardware that disappears into the architecture instead.
